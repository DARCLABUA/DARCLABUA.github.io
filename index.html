Security stack

Industry-standard tools, hands on

Our Implementations team works with the same security tools that companies run in production. Students don't just read about them. They install them, configure them and keep them running.

SIEM and XDR

Wazuh

Deploy agents to endpoints, collect and correlate logs, write detection rules and triage the alerts that come in.

Vulnerability scanning

Nessus

Plan and run scans, read findings by severity and track each one through remediation.

Endpoint protection

EDR and anti-malware

Roll out protection to machines, set policies and investigate detections when something gets flagged.

How students get there

  1. Learn the toolWhat it does, how it works and why organizations depend on it.
  2. Build it in the labStand it up from scratch in our own lab environment.
  3. Configure and tuneSet policies, rules and exclusions so alerts mean something.
  4. Run it for realWork toward implementing and maintaining it on real systems.

Proof of concept

Every tool earns its place with a written report

Before a tool goes into our environment, students research it and write a proof-of-concept report. The research looks at how the tool works, what it does well and which workflow gets the most out of it for our use case.

The report doubles as lab technical documentation. It details exactly how the tool fits into the environment, so anyone on the team can understand the setup, rebuild it or improve on it later.

D.A.R.C. Lab technical docsPoC report

What every report covers

  1. Research and use caseWhat the tool does, how it works and the workflow that best fits our use case.
  2. Environment fitExactly where it sits in the lab: which systems it touches and what it connects to.
  3. Implementation stepsA step-by-step install someone else can follow and repeat.
  4. Configuration and tuningThe settings we chose and why.
  5. Findings and recommendationWhat worked, what didn't and whether to roll it out.

Faculty sponsor

Dr. Raymond Partin

Dr. Partin is a criminologist whose research focuses on the Dark Web, with a special interest in the criminological differences between Dark Web users and non-users. He leads the Network Intrusion Lab and is Co-PI of the NSF Scholarship for Service (SFS) program at The University of Alabama.

  • Ph.D., International Crime and Justice, Florida International University, 2023
  • M.S. and B.S., Criminology and Criminal Justice, University of Cincinnati
  • Published in Crime & Delinquency, the Journal of Criminal Justice and the American Journal of Criminal Justice
