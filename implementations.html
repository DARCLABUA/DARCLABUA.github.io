Proof of concept

Every tool earns its place with a written report

Before a tool goes into our environment, students research it and write a proof-of-concept report. The research looks at how the tool works, what it does well and which workflow gets the most out of it for our use case.

The report doubles as lab technical documentation. It details exactly how the tool fits into the environment, so anyone on the team can understand the setup, rebuild it or improve on it later.

D.A.R.C. Lab technical docsPoC report

What every report covers

  1. Research and use caseWhat the tool does, how it works and the workflow that best fits our use case.
  2. Environment fitExactly where it sits in the lab: which systems it touches and what it connects to.
  3. Implementation stepsA step-by-step install someone else can follow and repeat.
  4. Configuration and tuningThe settings we chose and why.
  5. Findings and recommendationWhat worked, what didn't and whether to roll it out.
